## 1. Introduction & epidemiology
- Urinary system functions (A2, B2): filters blood & removes metabolic wastes; manages electrolyte concentrations; maintains acid-base balance; regulates fluid volume and blood pressure.
- Types of renal disorders (A3, B3): kidney failure (most common) [A] / kidney disease [B]; nephrolithiasis (kidney stones, renal calculi); UTIs ★"50% of all women" [B]; noncancerous enlargement of the prostate.
- ★CKD epidemiology (B4): >5–10% of world population; more than half of adults >70 years; 6.5% in Saudi Arabia.

## 2. Anatomy
- Urinary system (A4, B5): kidneys, ureters, urinary bladder, urethra (shorter in females than males).
- Kidney internal anatomy, 3 regions (A5, B6): cortex, medulla, (renal) pelvis. Figure labels: minor calyx, major calyx, renal medulla, renal cortex, renal pelvis.
- ★Kidney section figure (B9): nephron, renal hilum, renal cortex, renal medulla, renal column, renal pyramid, renal papilla, renal sinus, renal capsule, renal lobe, renal artery, renal vein.
- ★Path of urine drainage (B9) — SEQUENCE: collecting duct → papillary duct → minor calyx → major calyx → renal pelvis → ureter → urinary bladder.
- Nephrons (A6, B7): in the cortex; structural and functional units that form urine.
- Nephron composition, 5 parts (A7, B8): glomerulus; glomerular (Bowman) capsule; PCT; loop of Henle; DCT.
- ★Nephron figures (B10–B12): afferent arteriole, efferent arteriole, glomerulus, Bowman capsule, PCT, DCT, macula densa, collecting duct, descending limb, ascending limb, loop of Henle, vasa recta (capillaries), cortico-medullary junction, vein, artery, cortex, medulla, urine.
- Male genital system (A8–A10, B14–B16): closely related to urinary system; shares the urethra (conduit for urine, semen, other secretions); prostate surrounds urethra and, with seminal vesicles, secretes fluids into urethra; testes in scrotum create spermatozoa.
  - SEQUENCE (ejaculation): epididymis → vas deferens → ejaculatory ducts → urethra → exit.
  - Semen = sperm + fluid from seminal vesicles, bulbourethral (Cowper) glands, prostate.
- ★Male sagittal figure (B17): urethra, bulbourethral gland and duct, bulb of penis.

## 3. Patient assessment
- Scene size-up (A12, B19): standard precautions (avoid contact with urine); renal/genital conditions may mimic other abdominal problems; difficult to identify source of pain; sources: bacterial infection, extension of ureter by kidney stone, prostate enlargement [A]; ★"GU pain can have many origins" [B].
- Primary survey (A13, B20): general impression (★patient may exhibit extremes of activity); airway & breathing; circulation; transport decision (★consider how patient will be moved).
- History taking (A14, B21–22): history & physical exam provide info for management; ask the right questions; ★many diagnoses based on history; SAMPLE = Signs & symptoms, Allergies, Medications, Pertinent past history, Last oral intake, Events leading up.
- Pain (A15, B23): visceral, referred; OPQRST = Onset, Provocation, Quality, Region (radiation), Severity, Timing.
- Secondary assessment (A16–17, B24): focused exam, head to toe; abdomen: 4 quadrants (RUQ, LUQ, RLQ, LLQ) and 9 anatomic segments (R/L hypochondriac, epigastric, R/L lumbar, periumbilical, R/L iliac, hypogastric); monitor VS every 5 min; link abnormal signs to history; assess flank tenderness; visually inspect genitalia; ECG monitoring.
  - Quadrant figure labels: liver, gallbladder, iliac crest, cecum, appendix, costal arch, spleen, stomach, umbilicus, small intestine, colon, inguinal ligament, rectum, pubic symphysis.
- Reassessment (A18, B25): electrolyte imbalances → rapid organ deterioration; form field impression/treatment plan; serial VS ★at least every 5 min in possible renal failure.
- Pain management (A20, B26): ★after ABCs, position of comfort; nausea/vomiting possible; IV line, consider antiemetic; do not withhold pain control.

## 4. UTI (A22–24, B27–29)
- Definitive treatment = antibiotics; usually lower urinary tract; more common in women [A]; spread if untreated.
- Classic Sx: painful urination, frequent urges, difficulty urinating, pain; restless/uncomfortable; VS vary with degree of illness.
- Mx: supportive (ABCs); position of comfort; prepare for N/V; pyelonephritis or sepsis → more aggressive treatment; transport to nearest appropriate facility.

## 5. Urinary catheters (A25–27, B30)
- Foley catheter common in hospitalized urinary patients; latex/plastic tube to drainage bag; continuous outflow; measures urine output.
- Transport: urine backflow concern; do not lift drainage bag; do not pull out or kink; ensure internal balloon deflated before removal.

## 6. Urinary obstruction & incontinence (A28–29, B31–32)
- Retention = incomplete emptying or inability to empty bladder. Causes: kidney stones [A only], AKI, BPH, urethral obstructions, UTIs, nerve damage.
- Incontinence = loss of bladder control; medical problem in 2 categories: urge (sudden intense urge, involuntary loss within seconds–minutes); overflow (constant, continual slow flow).

## 7. Kidney stones (A30–35, B33–40)
- Originate in renal pelvis; form when excess insoluble salts or uric acid crystallize in urine.
- ★Risk factors: diet, hydration, family history, hypertension.
- 4 types: calcium (most common, may be hereditary); struvite (more common in women); uric acid; cystine.
- Assessment: almost always pain; [A] pain may increase BP and tachycardia; restless or guard abdomen; ★palpation may be difficult; VS vary; often hematuria; flank tenderness if stone obstructs ureter.
- Figure (A33, B36): hydronephrosis + hydroureter above stone/stricture vs normal kidney/ureter.
- Mx: pain relief, ★ABCs, position of comfort, analgesia per protocol, IV + fluids; ★hydration, pain control, antiemetics; ★alpha-blocker tamsulosin (Flomax) to increase urine flow.
- Some pass on their own; persistent: extracorporeal (shock wave) lithotripsy (ESWL); cystoscopy with stent; percutaneous nephrostomy (PCN) tube.

## 8. AKI (A36–43, B42–51)
- Sudden decrease in glomerular filtration; toxins accumulate. **Up to 50% [A] vs 20% [B] of critically ill develop AKI** (conflict). Mortality up to 70% in critical cases.
- Oliguria <500 mL/day adult; [A] <0.5 mL/kg/h adult or child. Anuria = complete cessation.
- May experience: generalized edema, acid buildup, high nitrogenous/metabolic wastes.
- Untreated → volume overload, hyperkalemia, uremia, metabolic acidosis.
- 3 types by location: prerenal (hypoperfusion; ★hepatorenal syndrome — advanced liver disease, renal perfusion ↓ from portal hypertension); ★intrarenal (glomerular, tubular, interstitial); postrenal (★blockage of urine flow from kidneys).
- Pathophys effects: impaired mentation, ★hypotension [B], fluid retention, tachycardia, acid-base imbalance, ↑PR and QT.
- ★Table 21-2 (B45): Prerenal — hypotension, tachycardia, dizziness, thirst. Intrarenal — flank pain, joint pain, oliguria, hypertension, headache, confusion, seizure. Postrenal — pain in lower flank/abdomen/groin/genitalia, oliguria, distended bladder, hematuria, peripheral edema.
- Findings: dehydration [A]; pale cool moist skin; ↓urine output or darkening; flank pain; AMS or HF signs if severe.
- Exam: abdomen for other causes / ascites; flank tenderness; ECG; signs of infection; VS for shock.
- Mx: ★metabolic changes life threatening; ABCs; IV bolus if shock; many have comorbidities.

## 9. CKD (A44–46, B52–54)
- Permanent loss of nephrons; scarring; uremia and azotemia; systemic complications.
- ALOC; lethargy, nausea, hypertension; pericarditis and pulmonary edema common.
- Not managed in field; higher risk pulmonary edema & cardiac disease; ★transport calmly.

## 10. ESRD (A47–50, B55–58)
- From untreated acute or chronic kidney disease; all function lost; toxins build up; fatal unless dialysis/transplant.
- After dialysis: weakness, dehydration. Missed dialysis: volume overload (★SOB, peripheral edema).
- S&S: uremic frost (photo B57), muscle twitching, coagulopathy, chest pain. Late: confusion, seizures, coma.
- Mx: dialysis or transplant only; ABCs; supportive care.

## 11. ★Renal failure care & hyperkalemia (B59–64, B83–89)
- 12-lead ECG to check electrolytes; monitor all VS and ETCO2; transport to hemodialysis-capable facility; fluids as necessary; treat dysrhythmias.
- 12-lead ECGs (B60–63, unlabeled): peaked T waves; wide QRS (hyperkalemia pattern — interpretation inferred, not captioned).
- Rx of ↑K+ (B64): 12-lead ECG; Ca++ stabilizes cardiac membranes → ↓arrhythmia risk, does NOT lower K+, avoid if digoxin toxicity suspected; reduce serum K: insulin + glucose, beta agonists, sodium bicarbonate "?".
- Case (B83–89): 64 y/o M, pre-syncope; ill and pale; airway patent, breathing normal; pale, prolonged cap refill, bradycardic; HR 52, BP 93/68, SpO2 95%, RR 18, T 36.5 °C; rhythm strip II (25 mm/s, 1 cm/mV) wide bizarre complexes/peaked T (no answer slide).

## 12. Dialysis (A51–55, B65–72)
- Purpose: filter toxic wastes, remove excess fluid, restore electrolyte balance. 2 types: peritoneal (CAPD, APD) and hemodialysis.
- Access: fistula or AV shunt (figures: fistula; looped graft).
- Hospital, community facility, or home; ★EMS mostly meets machines on transfers; every 2 or 3 days for 3–5 hours.
- Complications [A]: human error (cannulation of shunt; connection/disconnection); muscle cramps; N/V; infection at fistula/shunt; hypotension & shock; potassium imbalance; disequilibrium syndrome; air embolism. ★Missed dialysis → electrolyte imbalance.
- ★Problems may result from (B68): accidental disconnection; bleeding from fistula/shunt; machine malfunction; rapid fluid/electrolyte shifts → hypotension; potassium imbalances; disequilibrium syndrome.
- ★Disequilibrium syndrome (B69): progressive neuro S&S from cerebral edema due to fluid shifts into brain after relatively rapid ↓serum osmolality during HD; nausea, headache, disorientation, confusion, dizziness, seizures, coma/death.
- ★Table 21-3 Medical emergencies in dialysis patients (B72):
  - Dialysis itself: Hypotension → 50 mL NS IV. Hemorrhage from fistula/shunt → if cannot reconnect, clamp; direct pressure; check shock. Potassium imbalance → hypoK: treat bradycardia with atropine; hyperK: calcium and bicarbonate may be considered if lab value from sending facility. Disequilibrium → supportive only. Air embolism → left lateral recumbent, ~10° head-down. Machine dysfunction → turn off, clamp shunt ends, disconnect, transport.
  - More vulnerable to: Heart failure → O2, sitting, diuretic if producing urine, rapid transport to dialysis-capable facility. MI & dysrhythmias → treat as any patient, caution with medications. Hypertension → transport, supportive. Pericardial tamponade → emergency transport as soon as detected. Uremic pericarditis → O2, position of comfort, transport.

## 13. Male genital tract (A56–63, B74–82)
- Epididymitis: infection/inflammation of epididymis along posterior border of testis.
- Orchitis: one or both testes infected. Prehospital Mx supportive.
- Fournier gangrene: necrosis of subcutaneous tissue & muscle of scrotum; tender, warm, erythematous; tissues gray-black; aggressive IV fluids; prompt transport.
- Priapism: painful, tender, persistent erection; privacy; ★no assumptions about cause; respect; immobilize if SCI suspected; analgesics.
- Phimosis: inability to retract distal foreskin over glans; cold compress; transport.
- Paraphimosis: foreskin retracted over glans and entrapped; TRUE EMERGENCY; necrosis of glans.
- BPH: age-related nonmalignant enlargement; asymptomatic or difficulty starting flow, incomplete emptying, nocturia, retention.
- Testicular masses: rarely need prehospital Rx; painful or painless; most benign cystic masses or varicocele; cancer = painless solid lump.
- Testicular torsion: twisting on spermatic cord; emergency if blood flow reduced; usually unilateral; transport carefully & promptly; position of comfort; analgesics [A]. (Figure B82: twisted cord.)
